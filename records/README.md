# Records

## Purpose

This folder stores dated working records created from the thinking system.

Templates explain how to think. Records show what was prepared, captured, decided, revised, or paused for a specific meeting or review cycle.

## Record Rules

- Keep records plain Markdown.
- Do not store real patient data.
- Do not store identifying patient stories.
- Do not write diagnosis, triage, or treatment advice.
- Separate facts, assumptions, and decisions.
- Use reviewer statements carefully and mark inferences as inferences.
- Preserve safety boundaries even when a conversation becomes product-oriented.

## Naming Convention

Use:

- `YYYY-MM-DD/pre-meeting-brief.md`
- `YYYY-MM-DD/meeting-capture.md`
- `YYYY-MM-DD/decision-record.md`
- `YYYY-MM-DD/extraction-notes.md`
- `YYYY-MM-DD/<source-or-topic>-reference.md` for dated external reference material that changes product routing but is not a meeting capture.
- `YYYY-MM-DD/<demo-or-review>-readiness-record.md` for dated demo-readiness evidence that changes governance interpretation but not the safety boundary.
- `YYYY-MM-DD/<meeting-topic>-transcript.md` for corrected meeting transcripts assembled from ASR or notes.
- `YYYY-MM-DD/<meeting-topic>-summary-and-signals.md` for concise meeting summaries and strategic interpretation notes.

## Status Labels

Use one of:

- `draft`: prepared before a conversation
- `captured`: filled from conversation notes
- `synthesized`: compressed into a decision record
- `accepted`: reviewed and stable
- `superseded`: replaced by a later record
- `paused`: no further action until a blocker is resolved

## Audit Rule

A record is useful only if a reviewer can answer:

1. What did we know before the meeting?
2. What did we learn?
3. What changed?
4. What decision follows?
5. What must not be expanded yet?

# Next Step: Meeting-To-Decision Pack

## Purpose

The next step is to turn the thinking spec into a disciplined discovery workflow.

This step is not about building more product. It is about using the existing thinking layer to answer one question:

Does the urology smart-previsit concept solve a real workflow problem strongly enough to justify a next experiment?

## Target Outcome

After the physician meeting and follow-up summary, the project should have:

- a clear statement of the real clinic workflow problem
- a list of repeated questions worth pre-collecting
- a list of topics that must remain physician-led
- a first judgment on whether the guided previsit summary is useful
- a go, revise, or pause decision
- separate notes for paper framing, patent framing, and product decision-making

## Step 1: Pre-Meeting Readiness

### Goal

Enter the meeting with a clear discovery frame, not a product pitch.

### What To Bring

- the core problem statement
- the low-friction workflow diagram
- the physician question list
- the safety boundary
- the evaluation criteria
- the stop boundary

### Framing Sentence

Use this opening frame:

> The goal is not to replace physician judgment. The goal is to understand whether a guided previsit flow can collect repeated, high-value information before the formal visit and give the clinician a short reviewable summary.

### Meeting Boundary

Do not promise:

- diagnosis
- triage
- treatment suggestions
- hospital integration
- voice-first interview
- production deployment
- real patient-data use

### Discovery Questions

Ask these in order:

1. What happens now from patient check-in to physician entry?
2. Which questions are repeated by staff or physicians?
3. Which answers would be useful before the physician enters?
4. Which topics must remain physician-led?
5. Should the first useful workflow be self-filled, nurse-assisted, or mixed?
6. What summary format would be useful in under 60 seconds?
7. What information would be noise?
8. What would make this unsafe or unacceptable?
9. What metric would prove that the workflow helps?
10. What is the smallest useful next artifact?

## Step 2: Meeting Capture

### Capture Rule

Record observations in four separate categories:

| Category | What To Capture | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw workflow facts | current check-in, nursing, waiting, physician sequence | prevents imagined workflow design |
| Repeated questions | questions asked more than once | defines where previsit collection has value |
| Boundaries | physician-only topics, safety concerns, privacy concerns | prevents unsafe scope expansion |
| Decision signals | useful, noisy, unacceptable, next artifact | supports go / revise / pause decision |

### Avoid During Capture

- do not convert comments into product features immediately
- do not treat one clinician preference as universal truth
- do not smooth over objections
- do not hide workflow mismatch
- do not turn safety concerns into implementation tasks too early

## Step 3: Same-Day Synthesis

### Goal

Within the same day, compress the meeting into a one-page decision summary.

### One-Page Summary Structure

Use this structure:

| Section | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Clinical need | what problem exists before the physician-led visit |
| User need | what patients, nurses, or physicians need to reduce friction |
| Workflow pain points | where repeated questioning or missing information appears |
| Pre-collectable information | what can safely be asked before the physician enters |
| Physician-led information | what must remain physician-owned |
| Summary format | what the clinician would actually read |
| Safety concerns | what could mislead, overburden, or create privacy risk |
| MVP recommendation | continue, revise, or pause |
| Next owner/action | the smallest next artifact and who should review it |

### Synthesis Discipline

The summary should say what was learned, not what the team hopes is true.

Use cautious wording:

- observed
- reported
- requested
- likely useful
- may be noisy
- needs confirmation
- not yet justified

## Step 4: Decision Gate

### Continue

Continue only if:

- there is a real repeated-question problem
- clinicians can name useful pre-collected information
- the summary can be short enough to read
- safety boundaries remain acceptable
- the next artifact is small and concrete

### Revise

Revise if:

- the current question tree is too broad
- the summary format is wrong
- self-filled use is unrealistic but assisted use is plausible
- the main value is staff workflow, not patient self-entry
- the clinician wants different grouping or wording

### Pause

Pause if:

- the clinic has no place to use the workflow
- clinicians would not read the summary
- the concept adds staff burden without clear benefit
- safety or privacy concerns dominate
- the problem is better solved by existing intake forms

## Step 5: Paper Framing Extraction

### Paper-Relevant Claim

The paper framing should not be "AI asks medical questions."

The stronger framing is:

> A bounded clinician-review workflow can use guided previsit interaction to reduce repeated information gathering while preserving clinical authority and safety boundaries.

### Paper Angles

Use the thinking spec to support:

- human-centered clinical workflow design
- decision-boundary design
- safety-first AI system framing
- previsit information quality
- clinician-review summaries
- low-risk discovery before integration

### Evidence Needed

For a paper-quality argument, collect:

- current workflow map
- repeated-question examples
- clinician usefulness judgment
- safety objections
- summary format feedback
- go / revise / pause rationale

## Step 6: Patent Framing Extraction

### Patent-Relevant Invention Logic

The patent angle should not be a generic questionnaire.

The stronger invention logic is:

> A guided previsit workflow that separates patient-facing symptom capture, missing-information repair, safety-boundary preservation, and clinician-review summary generation.

### Potential Claim Areas

Possible non-final claim areas:

- separating patient input from clinician interpretation
- identifying missing previsit information before handoff
- generating neutral clinician-review flags instead of diagnostic claims
- supporting self-filled, nurse-assisted, and family-assisted intake modes
- producing a constrained summary designed for rapid clinical review

### Patent Caution

Do not overclaim novelty until prior art is reviewed.

Use the thinking repo to clarify the logic, not to assert patentability.

## Step 7: Product Decision Extraction

### Product Question

The product question is:

Would a real clinic adopt this because it saves time or improves readiness without adding burden?

### Product Decision Matrix

| Decision | Use When | Next Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue | workflow pain is real and summary is useful | refine question tree and summary |
| Revise | concept useful but current format wrong | redesign around clinician feedback |
| Narrow | only one patient group or symptom group is valuable | focus the MVP |
| Pause | no workflow fit or too much burden | stop product work and preserve learning |

### Product Anti-Goals

Do not turn the next product step into:

- a full app roadmap
- hospital integration planning
- voice AI exploration
- clinical decision automation
- data platform design

## Step 8: Repository Update After Meeting

After the meeting, update this thinking repo with:

- confirmed workflow facts
- rejected assumptions
- revised open questions
- decision gate result
- paper/patent/product extraction notes

Do not add real patient data.

Do not add implementation details.

Do not rewrite the entire thinking spec unless the meeting invalidates a core assumption.

## Acceptance Criteria

This next step is complete when:

- the meeting has a one-page summary
- each open question is answered, revised, or marked unknown
- the continue / revise / pause decision is explicit
- safety boundaries are unchanged or updated deliberately
- paper, patent, and product implications are separated
- no real patient data is stored
- no technical implementation plan replaces workflow reasoning

## Minimal Work Plan

If time is limited, do only this:

1. Ask the ten discovery questions.
2. Capture repeated questions and physician-only boundaries.
3. Write the one-page summary.
4. Decide continue, revise, or pause.
5. Record one paper angle, one patent angle, and one product decision.

# Workflow Logic

## Workflow Purpose

The workflow exists to prepare the visit, not complete the visit.

The system is useful only if it fits a real clinical sequence: arrival, waiting, possible staff contact, physician review, and formal consultation. If there is no practical moment to use it, the concept should be revised or paused.

## Encounter Sequence

The intended encounter logic is:

1. patient arrives or prepares for the visit
2. patient, staff member, or helper begins the previsit flow
3. patient identifies the main concern
4. patient answers structured questions
5. missing key information is identified
6. patient, staff member, or helper reviews the answers
7. a clinician-review summary is prepared
8. clinician confirms, edits, ignores, or asks follow-up questions

This sequence is deliberately incomplete. It stops before diagnosis, triage, and treatment decisions.

## Decision Points

## Who Completes The Flow?

The system must support self-filled and assisted use because patient ability varies. The workflow should not assume that every patient can read small text, type comfortably, understand medical terms, or use a phone without help.

## What Gets Asked?

Only repeated, high-value, previsit-safe information should be asked. The question tree should be revised if physicians say a field is irrelevant, too vague, too sensitive, or better handled in person.

## What Happens When Information Is Missing?

Missing information should be surfaced before the summary. The goal is not to force every answer. The goal is to make gaps visible so the clinician does not mistake the summary for a complete history.

## What Happens Before Handoff?

Patient or helper review is required because answers may be misunderstood, skipped, or entered by someone else. Review protects both usefulness and trust.

## What Does The Clinician Receive?

The clinician receives a short review summary. The summary should be neutral, editable in spirit, and easy to ignore if it is not useful.

## Discovery Questions

The workflow must be tested with the physician or clinic team:

1. What happens now between check-in and physician entry?
2. Which questions are repeated most often?
3. Which answers would be useful before the physician enters?
4. Which topics must remain physician-led?
5. Who should operate the workflow: patient, nurse, family, or mixed?
6. What summary format would be read in under one minute?
7. What information would be noise?
8. What wording would be unsafe?
9. What metric would prove value?
10. What is the smallest useful next artifact?

## Workflow Acceptance Rule

The workflow is acceptable only if it reduces repeated questioning or missing context without adding unacceptable burden, unsafe confidence, or unclear responsibility.
